Engineering Vector Theorems: Stokes' & Divergence

First-Principles Guide, Equation Proof & Fully Solved Mathematical Example

$$\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot d\mathbf{S}$$
1. Plain-English Meaning & Intuitive Analogy
Stokes' Theorem states that adding up all the vector force pushes along a closed outer boundary curve (\(C\)) yields the exact same total result as summing up all the tiny microscopic rotational swirls (Curl \(\nabla \times \mathbf{F}\)) across the entire enclosed surface (\(S\)).

💡 The Fishing Net Analogy: Imagine placing a circular net (Surface \(S\)) bounded by a metal outer ring (Boundary \(C\)) into a swirling river:
  • Left-Hand Side (\(\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}\)): Walk around the solid outer metal ring \(C\) and measure how much the water pushes you forward along your path.
  • Right-Hand Side (\(\iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot d\mathbf{S}\)): Place tiny paddlewheels in every mesh square across the net \(S\). Adjacent paddlewheels push against each other and cancel their internal spins out! The only uncancelled spins are along the outer border, which equal the force measured along the ring.

3. Fully Solved Step-by-Step Mathematical Example
Problem: Verify Stokes' Theorem for \(\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = -y\mathbf{i} + x\mathbf{j} + z\mathbf{k}\) over a circular path \(C\) of radius \(R = 2\) on plane \(z=0\).
📌 Step A: Calculate Left-Hand Side (LHS) Line Integral \(\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}\)
1. Parameterize curve \(C\): \(\mathbf{r}(t) = 2\cos(t)\mathbf{i} + 2\sin(t)\mathbf{j} + 0\mathbf{k}, \quad t \in [0, 2\pi]\)
2. Path differential \(d\mathbf{r}\): \(d\mathbf{r} = (-2\sin(t)\mathbf{i} + 2\cos(t)\mathbf{j} + 0\mathbf{k})\,dt\)
3. Evaluate \(\mathbf{F}\) on \(C\): \(\mathbf{F} = -2\sin(t)\mathbf{i} + 2\cos(t)\mathbf{j} + 0\mathbf{k}\)
4. Dot Product \(\mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}\): \([(-2\sin t)(-2\sin t) + (2\cos t)(2\cos t)]\,dt = 4(\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t)\,dt = 4\,dt\)
5. Integration: \(\int_{0}^{2\pi} 4\,dt = [4t]_{0}^{2\pi} = \mathbf{8\pi \approx 25.1327}\)

📌 Step B: Calculate Right-Hand Side (RHS) Surface Integral \(\iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot d\mathbf{S}\)
1. Calculate Curl \(\nabla \times \mathbf{F}\):
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial x}{\partial z}\right)\mathbf{i} - \left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial (-y)}{\partial z}\right)\mathbf{j} + \left(\frac{\partial x}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial (-y)}{\partial y}\right)\mathbf{k} = \mathbf{2\mathbf{k}}$$ 2. Surface area element: \(d\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{k}\,dA\) (normal vector points straight up)
3. Surface integral: \(\iint_S (2\mathbf{k}) \cdot (\mathbf{k}\,dA) = 2 \iint_S dA\)
4. Circle area with \(R=2\): \(\text{Area} = \pi R^2 = 4\pi\)
5. Total Value = \(2(4\pi) = \mathbf{8\pi \approx 25.1327}\)

✅ Verification: LHS (\(8\pi\)) = RHS (\(8\pi\)). Stokes' Theorem holds true!
